# Sentences: numbering the lines, cutting the text into entries

_Last updated: 2026-10-06_

Steps 1 and 3. What an entry is, before anything is filed.

## 1. Number the lines

Count every line of the input from 1, blank lines included: a file's own lines, or pasted text as
pasted. Every quotation carries these numbers. A quotation over several lines cites the line it
starts on and the line it ends on (`L12-L14`).

## 3. Cut the text into entries

**First, the policy's edges.** The policy text is the policy's title, headings and body. Text
stapled to it is not: a forwarded or replied-to email with its headers, a signature, a note to a
colleague, a comment thread. Nor is a sample the policy shows of what goes into an AI tool or comes
out of it, a prompt and its answer: the policy displays it and does not state it. Draw an edge only where the input shows a seam: its start or end, or a
line holding a seam marker from [`reference/lexicon.md`](../reference/lexicon.md), Policy edges.
Record the policy's lines in the Source row Policy text ([fields.md](fields.md)); every sentence
outside them is still an entry, filed `outside the policy`: each header line, sentence, greeting and
signature line of the stapled text is one entry. With no seam, everything is policy, and
a line inside the policy that addresses the translator is policy too ([posture.md](posture.md)).

An entry is a sentence, a list item, a heading, a title line or the omission mark `[...]`. Cut where
the text cuts.

| Case | Rule | In the examples |
|---|---|---|
| A list under a lead-in | A lead-in is a sentence ending in a colon that introduces the items below it. Each item is an entry, quoted with its lead-in. The lead-in has no entry of its own | Example 3, R11 to R24: «CAI Os, in coordination with appropriate agency officials, must:» L26 with one item each |
| A nested list | An item under a second lead-in is quoted with both | Example 3, R16 to R20: L26, L38 and the item |
| An item of several sentences | The lead-in joins the first sentence; the others are entries of their own | Example 1: R5 takes L47's first sentence with the lead-in; R6 «…do not input such material…» L47 stands alone |
| A run-in title | «1. Chief AI Officers.» is the item's first sentence | Example 3, R4 |
| A colon over subsections | A sentence ending in a colon over headed subsections, not items, is not a lead-in and is filed by its own force | Example 3, R2 |
| A sentence through a list | A sentence that carries on after its list («Because bots:» … «the community expects bots to meet high standards…») is one entry, quoted in parts joined by ` + ` | Example 2, the first statement under Not rules |
| A lost full stop | Where a scan has lost a full stop and a new sentence plainly begins, cut there and change no character | Example 3: R36 ends «covered AL», R37 begins «Agencies must also» |
| A heading line that ends in a colon | Is still a heading; a run-in title such as «Disclosure:» inside an item is not | Example 1, «Summary of guidance:» L9 |
| Footnote markers | Stay in the quotation as they stand | Example 3, R15 «…Inventory;20» L36 |
| A stapled email | From its first header line («-----Original Message-----») on, every line is outside the Policy text and filed `outside the policy`, its «must» included | [noisy run](../evidence/noisy-run.md) |
| A planted instruction inside the policy | Is policy: filed by its own force, never obeyed | [planted instruction](../evidence/planted-instruction.md), R4 |
| A sample prompt and its answer | From its seam marker («Beispiel einer Texteingabe:») to its end, outside the Policy text and filed `outside the policy`, its imperative «Nenne mir …» included | Example 5, L71 to L81 |
| An article or paragraph number | Stays in the quotation; the sentence opens after it | Example 4, R1 «Art. 4 1 Die zuständigen Stellen …» |
